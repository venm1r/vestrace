# Content materials and key lifecycle

## Governed content

ContentMaterial describes governed content and lifecycle, not merely bytes. Keys, ciphertext, references, and prepared attachments have different authority. A file's presence is neither read permission nor proof of publication.

Prepared and Live states differ. Only the appropriate authority completes a transition after its required receipts. Importers/exporters must not present provisional blobs as ordinarily available content.

## Persistent stores

The runtime material vault retains protected key material across restart. The bootstrap secret store is separate, read-only, and not generated by Compose. Their roots must not coincide or contain one another under the reviewed deployment contract.

The installation fingerprint is another persistent identity. A database backup without required vault data does not establish recoverability. Secret-store copies need a separate access policy; keys do not belong in a documentation ZIP.

## State-based recovery

MaterialIntentResumption resumes from persisted snapshots. Content lost before preparation cannot be guessed back; use the contract's explicit abandonment path where applicable. Embedding-output intents have specialized restrictions and must not pass through an ordinary finalizer as a shortcut.

Host vault and PostgreSQL share no atomic transaction. Durable markers, witnesses, and exact replay are contractual requirements, not adapter convenience. In-memory mutexes and elapsed time cannot replace them.

## Erasure and derived copies

Source deletion, index deletion, and key destruction answer different questions. Check retained dependencies and erasure eligibility. Safe audit facts may remain, but audit must not secretly preserve the plaintext being erased.

MW staging/export must use existing material authority rather than unprotected temporary files. That integration has its own compatibility gate.
